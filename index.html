Who we are

Smart Drug Delivery Computing is an Australian software company turning mechanistic modelling into practical tools for nanomedicine and drug delivery design.

Whether a drug reaches its target depends on processes that happen at scales experiments struggle to see — particle–cell and particle–matrix interactions, barrier crossing, retention, degradation and drug release. Our software makes these hidden mechanisms visible and quantitative.

Alongside our simulation platform, we offer custom software development, simulation services, scientific advice and computational consulting.

About us
The challenge

The mechanisms that decide delivery success are hidden at the microscale.

Hidden mechanisms

Particle-scale interactions, local forces and mass transfer occur at spatial and temporal scales that conventional in vitro and in vivo experiments cannot resolve directly.

Limits of experiments

Animal studies are costly and raise ethical concerns, while organoids and organ-on-chip models still cannot measure key particle-scale information on their own.

AI without the right data

AI is powerful when relevant data exist — but the microscale transport data that matter most for nanomedicine design are largely missing.

Our approach

Physics empowers AI. AI makes design predictive.

We use physics-based modelling to generate the missing microscale information, calibrate it against experimental and imaging data, then use AI to learn from it, generalise it and turn it into actionable design rules.

01 · Physics reveals

Mechanistic multiscale models

Particle-scale and multiscale models capture convection, diffusion, adhesion, aggregation, cellular uptake, barrier crossing, breakage, degradation and drug release.

02 · AI learns

Physics-informed AI

Physically consistent simulation data train surrogate and reduced-order models for rapid prediction, sensitivity analysis and uncertainty quantification.

03 · Design guidance

Predictive design rules

Mechanisms become recommendations for particle size, charge, coating, stiffness, drug loading and release kinetics — before you go to the lab.
